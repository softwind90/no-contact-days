What to text your ex: short templates, and when not to send.

Looking for what to text an ex, a text to send your ex boyfriend, or what to say to an ex? Start here: most of the time you should not send anything. If you still have one real reason, copy a short apology, calm check-in, or closure note — then stop.

Most of the time, you should not send it

What should I text my ex? Often the honest answer is nothing tonight. A message feels useful when you are lonely, ashamed, or waiting for proof that the relationship still matters. That feeling is real. It is not the same as a reason to hit send.

Send a text only when all of this is true: you know the one purpose, you can keep it short, and you can live with silence. If any of those fail, leave the draft unsent and open the 60-second pause. If you are in no contact, protect the streak on the No Contact Tracker instead of opening the chat.

This page is a message library. The page I want to text my ex is the urge tool: write it privately, wait a minute, then decide. Do not pick a prettier sentence while the urge is still making the decision.

Five texts you can copy, grouped by reason

These are templates, not scripts for getting a reaction. Change the detail so it is true. Send one message for one purpose. The same shape works if you are writing an ex-boyfriend, an ex-girlfriend, or any former partner. What to say to an ex out loud follows the same rule: one point, then stop talking.

Apology

An apology names what you did. It does not ask them to forgive you, comfort you, or reopen the relationship.

“I’ve been thinking about how I spoke in that last argument. I’m sorry I raised my voice. I’m not asking for a conversation. I wanted to say that clearly.”

“I’m sorry I kept pushing for an answer after you asked for space. That wasn’t fair. You don’t need to reply.”

Calm check-in

A check-in is a short, low-pressure note. If you will feel crushed by no reply, it is not calm yet — wait, or use the guide to texting without looking desperate before you edit it again.

“Hi. I’ve had some space and I’m doing all right. I hope you are too. No need to write back.”

“I found your jacket while packing. I can leave it with the front desk on Saturday between 2 and 4. No need to meet.”

Closure

Closure you can actually keep does not depend on their answer. Say you are stopping, wish them well, and do not add a question.

“I’m going to stop reaching out so this can settle. I wish you well, and I don’t need a reply for that to be true.”

How to use one of these texts

  • One purpose. Apology, check-in, closure, or a practical detail. Not all of them in the same bubble.
  • Send once. Silence is an answer. A second text that says “just checking you saw this” restarts the loop.
  • Cut the extra sentence. If a line exists to make them feel guilty, jealous, or responsible for your mood, delete it.
  • Respect a no. If they asked for no contact, do not send a personal message. Logistics can stay on whatever channel you already agreed to use.

If you are ending contact rather than reopening it, the last text before no contact is the closer fit. If a no-contact window is already underway, read first text after no contact before you treat a milestone as permission.

Use this filter before you copy anything

  • Is it necessary? Logistics, money, housing, safety, kids, or a specific apology you can offer without a reply can be valid.
  • Is it calm? If you are shaking, angry, drinking, or bargaining, the wording will not save the moment. Wait.
  • Is it short? A clear text is not a breakup essay.
  • Can you accept no reply? If silence would send you back to the thread tonight, do not send it today.

More examples when the reason is practical

The five texts above cover apology, check-in, and closure. Use these only when the situation is concrete. Still one message, still no chase.

Shared logistics

“Can you confirm the utility amount by Friday? I’ll send my share after that.”

“I can collect the last boxes Saturday between 10 and 11. Tell me if that window does not work.”

A boundary

“I’m not ready to be friends. I’m stepping back from personal conversations. Please contact me only about the lease.”

“Please keep messages to the parenting schedule. I’ll reply about the kids.”

A reply that does not reopen the breakup

“I got your message. I’m not ready to talk about the relationship. I hope you’re well.”

“I can answer the practical question. I’m not available to revisit the breakup.”

Texts to leave unsent

Skip anything whose real job is to pull a feeling out of them: “Do you miss me?”, “Was any of it real?”, “I just need closure”, “Can we talk one last time?”, or “I heard something about you.” Those can feel urgent and still be a test.

Also skip fake emergencies, jealousy, “accidental” messages, a stack of apologies, and a second text because the first one feels too short. If you need the words out of your body, write them as an unsent letter and keep them there.

If you are already in no contact

The end of 14, 30, or 60 days is a review, not a send button. Check how long to keep no contact before you treat the date as permission. If the message is not necessary, count the day on the no contact calculator and go back to the tracker.

If you do send one necessary note, do not turn it into a thread. One purpose, one send, then return to the boundary you chose.

Frequently asked questions

What should I text my ex?

Most of the time, nothing. Send a text only when you have one clear purpose, such as a brief apology, a calm check-in you can send without needing a reply, a closure note, or a practical detail. Keep it short and send it once.

What to text your ex if you want them to reply?

A text written to force a reply usually reads as pressure. If you need the answer in order to feel okay, do not send it yet. Use a pause first, then decide whether any message is necessary.

What is a calm text to send an ex boyfriend or ex girlfriend?

The same shape works for an ex-boyfriend, ex-girlfriend, or any former partner: one purpose, a few plain sentences, no demand for reassurance, and no second text if they stay quiet.

Should I text my ex for closure?

Only if you can handle no reply or an answer you do not like. A closure text states that you are stopping, takes responsibility for your part, and does not ask them to comfort you.

What should I not text my ex?

Skip guilt, jealousy, essays, vague bait, repeated apologies, and questions that ask them to prove they still care. Also skip a follow-up sent only because the first message felt too quiet.

Where do I decide whether to send, instead of choosing words?

Use the 60-second pause on I want to text my ex. This page is a message library. That page is the urge tool. Choose words only after you have decided a message is actually worth sending.
