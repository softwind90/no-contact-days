The urge peaks, then it changes

Wanting to text your ex can feel like a command: send it now or the feeling will stay. In practice the urge rises, gets loud, and eases if you do not open the thread. The relief of sending often lasts minutes. The reset — checking, hoping, replaying — can last days.

Common peaks: late at night, after a drink, after their name shows up, a song, a dream, a birthday, or the thought that one answer would finally settle you. If a profile started it, read how to stop checking your ex’s social media. If you want the “why” in more detail, see why the urge shows up.

Do not open the chat app first. The thread makes sending feel like the next tiny step.

Name the peak. Loneliness, panic, anger, hope, alcohol, or a date on the calendar. A named urge is easier to wait out than a mystery.

Give it one minute. You are not deciding the whole breakup. You are deciding not to act at the top of the wave.

A 60-second flow

Use the box on this page. Nothing you type here is saved or sent. If you want to keep the words, copy them somewhere private before you leave.

  1. Stay out of the texting app.
  2. If it helps, type the message you are not sending. Do not polish it into something more convincing.
  3. Name the trigger in a few words.
  4. Start the 60-second timer and take a few slow breaths.
  5. When it ends, read what you wrote. Then check the day on the No Contact Tracker or the no contact calculator.

After the minute, ask one question: is this message necessary, or is it trying to buy comfort? If it can wait, leave it here and revisit tomorrow. Morning is a fairer editor than midnight.

When it might be okay to send

A text can be a reasonable next step when the reason would still make sense if you felt calm. That usually means a practical responsibility, not a mood.

  • There is a real detail to handle: kids, housing, money, a pet, safety, or something you still share.
  • You can say it in a few sentences without explaining the whole relationship.
  • You can accept silence, a short reply, or a colder reply than you hope for.
  • They have not asked you to stop, or this is the channel you already use for logistics only.

If those are true, decide first, then go to the text examples and copy one short message. Wording for a first note after a quiet stretch is on first text after no contact. A goodbye before you start the boundary is on last text before no contact. Do not browse those pages while you are still trying to soothe the peak.

When not to send

  • You want the reply to prove they care, miss you, or regret the breakup.
  • You are angry, drunk, or bargaining, and the text is how you plan to come down.
  • You already sent one message and the silence feels unbearable. That is a second urge, not a new reason.
  • A no-contact date arrived and you are treating it as permission. Read how long no contact lasts before you use the calendar as a green light.
  • They asked for no contact. Practical exceptions still stay practical. The no contact rule is a boundary, not a pause until you find the right sentence.

If the draft fails this list, keep it unsent. Prompts for a private letter are in unsent letter examples. If you are unsure after the pause, the should I text my ex checklist is the slower version of the same decision.

This page does not supply the message

People land here wanting both a decision and a script. Splitting them helps. Decide on this page. If — and only if — you still have a necessary reason once you are calmer, open what to text an ex for a short apology, check-in, closure note, or logistics line. For tone, how to text your ex without looking desperate covers length and double-texting. Neither page is a reason to send.

If they texted you first

You do not owe a reply at the speed the notification arrived. Read it once, step away, and sort it: practical, emotional, or mixed. Practical notes can get one brief answer. Emotional notes can wait until your body is quieter. Use what to do if your ex texts you if you need a reply filter.

If you already sent it

One message is not a reason to send three more. Stop the thread, name what triggered you, and start the next clean stretch from here. The broke no contact guide is the reset. Then open the tracker and count from the choice you are making now, not from the story of the slip.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I want to text my ex?

Do not open the chat first. Name the urge, write the message somewhere private if that helps, and wait 60 seconds before you decide. Most urges peak and then drop if you do not feed them.

What to do if you want to text your ex at night?

Night makes loneliness louder. Do not decide from the peak. Write the message outside the texting app, run a 60-second pause, and revisit it in the morning if it still matters.

When might it be okay to text an ex?

A message can be reasonable when it is about a real responsibility, you are calm, it stays short, and you can accept no reply. If the point is relief, reassurance, or a test, wait.

When should I not text my ex?

Do not text to stop anxiety, to check whether they care, to get closure you need them to provide, or because a no-contact date arrived. Also do not text if they asked you not to, except for agreed practical matters.

Where are examples of what to text an ex?

Copy-paste examples live on what to text an ex. Use them only after you have decided a message is necessary. This page is the pause, not a template list.

What if I already sent the message?

Do not turn one message into a thread. Stop, name the trigger, and restart the boundary from the next choice. The reset guide walks through that without turning the slip into a verdict.
