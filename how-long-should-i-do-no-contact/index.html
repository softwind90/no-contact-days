How long should no contact last: 14, 30, or 60 days?

Compare three review windows, see when to extend or shorten one, and pick a length you can actually keep. None of these dates is an order to text your ex.

The short answer

Start with 30 days if you are unsure. Use 14 days as a first stability checkpoint when a longer promise feels impossible. Consider 60 days when every interaction strongly restarts anxiety, hope, checking, or bargaining. At the end of the window, review — do not treat the date as permission to reach out.

The no contact rule is the boundary. This page is only about how long to try the first stretch. Track it on the No Contact Tracker or count an existing streak with the calculator.

Choose the length for your own stability

No contact is for your clarity, not for steering their feelings. There is no number of days that reliably makes an ex return, and this page will not pretend there is. If your only metric is whether they text, every quiet day will feel like a failure. Track what is changing for you: fewer impulsive opens of the thread, a bit more sleep, less checking, a decision you can stand behind the next morning.

When to extend the window

Extend when the original date is approaching and you can tell the plan is “text them,” not “review how I am.” That is information, not an emergency. Consider more time if:

  • You are still writing long explanations or emotional tests, even if you have not sent them.
  • You check their social media for clues more than you check your own day.
  • One message still feels like the thing that would fix the pain.
  • Seeing their name spikes you hard enough that a reply would run the next day.

Extending is not a verdict that you are behind. It is the same boundary, kept a little longer because the reason for it is still active. If the urge is loud today, use the 60-second pause instead of renegotiating the whole timeline at the peak.

When to shorten it

Shorten total silence when the original plan ignores real life. Shared children, a home, money, safety, legal issues, or work can make “no messages at all” dishonest. In that case, switch to limited contact: brief, factual, and stuck to the practical issue. Do not use the logistics as a doorway back into the breakup.

Do not shorten the window only because a night is lonely, a birthday is coming, or you found a polite sentence. Those are urges. A calmer length is the one you can keep without scaring yourself into it. Nothing bad is guaranteed to happen if you text on day 12. Something predictable often happens: the loop restarts, and you have less information about what you are like without it. If you are considering a message anyway, decide on first text after no contact rather than quietly moving the finish line.

What to do on the review date

  1. Look at the streak on the tracker and the calculator. The number is a checkpoint.
  2. Ask whether you are steadier than when you started, not whether they miss you.
  3. If you want to write, run the pause first. Copy a line from what to text an ex only after the answer is yes.
  4. If the answer is not yet, pick the next window on purpose and keep counting. You do not need a dramatic reason. “I am not ready to hear silence” is enough.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I do no contact?

A common first window is 30 days. Use 14 days when you need a shorter stability checkpoint, and consider 60 days when every interaction strongly restarts anxiety, hope, or checking. These are review points, not rules that say you must text when the date arrives.

Is 30 days of no contact enough?

Thirty days is enough to create space and notice patterns. It is not proof that you are healed or that reaching out is wise. At day 30, review whether you are calmer and whether any message is actually necessary. The day 30 guide is a checkpoint, not a finish line.

When should I extend no contact?

Consider a longer window if you still draft emotional tests, check their social media for clues, or would feel crushed by silence. Extend it as a review of your own stability, not as a punishment or a scare tactic.

When should I shorten no contact?

Shorten total silence when shared kids, housing, money, safety, or legal matters make it unrealistic. Switch to limited, practical contact. Do not shorten the boundary just because a day feels lonely or because you hope a text will bring them back.

Can I do no contact if we share responsibilities?

Yes, as limited contact. Keep messages brief and restricted to children, housing, finances, safety, legal matters, or other necessary logistics. The exceptions are spelled out in the no contact rule guide.
